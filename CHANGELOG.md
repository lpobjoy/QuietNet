# Changelog

## Unreleased

The relay moved to `quiet-net-relay.q3n.net` (25 November):
- **Changed: the relay's sentences name `quiet-net-relay.q3n.net`**, where it is now reached, in place of `q3n.net`, which no longer leads to it: the seven that send people somewhere to renew, to sort out a payment, to contact the seller, to remove a Pi, or that say a Pi is not connected. The name is written once, as `RELAY_NAME` in `cloud/relay.py`, and a test fails if a sentence has it typed in.
- A Pi paired while the relay was at `q3n.net` keeps that address and must be paired again: on the relay's page, name it under "Add a QuietNet", press "Create pairing code", and run the one line shown on the Pi. The Pi's old entry is replaced, and its rules and history are not affected.

Faults found in a check of the six parts of the IPv6 design joined together (fixed 24 November; the table is "Found in the check of the joined IPv6 build" in `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`):
- **Fixed: several devices behind one hardware address could all get one device's rules over IPv6.** A Wi-Fi range extender behind which only one device uses QuietNet over IPv4 is never seen at two numbers at once, so it was never noticed, and every IPv6 address under its hardware address was given to that one device: a device the owner had left out could be blocked by a child's "internet off" or bedtime once it asked over IPv6. A hardware address the IPv6 neighbour table lists with more than one link-local address (each device has only one) is now matched to no device and to no number over IPv6 (`several_link_local()`, `S.v6_unclear`: its lookups are refused while any device is treated differently, otherwise answered under whole-house rules and filed under their own address, each with a share of the listeners of its own), the same test IPv6 steering already used, and the announcement trial holds back while there is one, saying why. Manual step 78 has the case.
- **Fixed: IPv6 steering could move its claim to a device the owner never chose.** It sent the claim to whichever link-local address was last heard from a hardware address. It now keeps every one heard for an hour; with more than one known for a hardware address (heard, listed by the neighbour table, or last claimed) it leaves the device alone, corrects any claim already made and says so on the device's page. The neighbour table's address comes first; a heard one is used only when it gives none. Manual step 65.
- **New: the announcement trial holds back while the home router hands out IPv6 addresses by DHCPv6.** The trial's message has the two DHCPv6 flags clear, which says "not used here", and a Windows computer is reported to give its DHCPv6 address up when a second announcer says that. QuietNet now reads both flags from each real router's announcements (`managed` and `other` under `ipv6.routers` in `GET /api/status`) and holds the trial back, with the reason on the page, while a router heard in the last hour sets the first. The message itself is unchanged. Issue 102 says what is left with only the second flag set; new manual step 82.
- **Fixed (wording): the trial's switch and the docs now give the joined code's true reasons for both of its holds.** For a range extender: lookups would be refused or given only whole-house rules ("another's rules" only on data not yet moved). For two link-local addresses on the Pi's card: answers are sent from the address that was asked, and the hold is kept only because that has not been tried on a Pi with two. The switch also names the two new holds.
- **Fixed (docs): "a steered device's IPv6 DNS is never left silent" was true only of lookups sent to the hub's IPv6 DNS address.** A lookup a steered device sends to any other IPv6 DNS server (one built into a Chromecast or Google TV) is not caught, as the design specifies, and is dropped with the device's other IPv6; its IPv4 lookups are still answered. Issue 101 and new manual step 83 cover it. No behaviour changed.
- **Fixed (docs): three places still said nothing acts on the routers heard over IPv6.** The optional IPv6 steering takes the most recently heard one as the hub, not yet checked against the hub's IPv4 hardware address (issue 101); `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/API.md` and the docstring now say so.
- **Fixed (docs): known issue 79 and the "Not covered" list still described rules moving to a phone's new private Wi-Fi address within a minute;** they now describe the "Is it the same device?" question.
- Docs: issue 5 and the opening of `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` now mention the later parts of the IPv6 design (issues 97 to 102), the manual step ranges read 18 to 28 and 49 to 60, "Not covered" in `docs/TESTING.md` lists IPv6 steering and Merge and Separate, and the file table in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` gives the dashboard page's real length.

IPv6 DNS announcement trial (sixth part of the IPv6, DNS and device identity design, option (b); 24 November):
- **New: a trial, off unless switched on, "Announce QuietNet as an IPv6 DNS server"** (`announcer.py`; `POST /api/dns-announce-switch`; `dns_announce` in the status; on "Set up a device", tagged "Trial"). While it is on, the Pi tells every device on the home network, about every 200 seconds, that it can be used for IPv6 DNS for the next 10 minutes, at the Pi's own link-local address. It cannot replace the hub's announcement, so it does not stop any device getting round QuietNet, and the page says so; no "Can bypass over IPv6" label changes because of it.
- **It can't make the Pi anyone's router.** The message has a router lifetime of 0, no address range, no route, no DHCPv6 flags and no timers: nothing but the DNS part, naming only the address it is sent from. The whole frame is built byte for byte with a correct checksum, and a hardware address or address that fails its check stops it being built.
- **It announces only while QuietNet really answers over IPv6.** Before every announcement, and every 20 seconds between them, QuietNet asks itself a test question at the address it is about to name, on port 53. No answer, and devices are told to stop at once; the page says "Switched on, but not announcing" and why.
- **Devices are told to stop** (the same message with a lifetime of 0, three times) when the trial is switched off, on a clean stop (`docker stop`, Ctrl-C), and when QuietNet stops answering. After a power cut there is no goodbye: devices drop the Pi when the 10 minutes run out.
- Docs: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` has a new part, "The IPv6 DNS announcement trial", with the frame, the timings and the choices made; `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` has new open issue 102 (what the trial cannot do and what is untried); `docs/TESTING.md` has manual steps 69 to 81 for a real Pi and hub. New test file `tests/test_announcer.py`.
- **Fixed before release (a check of the trial; each fault is in the Fixed list of `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`):**
  - **The switch now says that devices you never set up to use QuietNet may start using it.** The announcement goes to every device; one that takes it up gets your whole-house rules, the rules of the group new devices are put in, advert blocking and a place in the history. The switch, the README and issue 102 say so, and what to do if a device that was left out stops working; issue 102 no longer calls the hub-off case harmless. Wording only; new manual step 77.
  - **The trial holds back while a Wi-Fi range extender is known.** Over IPv6 every device behind one is still judged under one device's rules, and the trial would have invited them all to ask that way, so one could be cut by another's bedtime. New `announce_hold_reason()` in `server.py`, handed to the announcer as `hold_reason`; the page and the status give the reason. Manual step 78.
  - **The trial holds back where the Pi would be the only IPv6 DNS server named while a router hands out IPv6.** There every goodbye, the off switch included, would take away an Android phone's only IPv6 DNS server, and Android answers by dropping off Wi-Fi. It announces only when a real router heard in the last hour names an IPv6 DNS server that is not the Pi, or when it has listened for 2,000 seconds (`ANNOUNCE_AFTER_QUIET`) and no real router was heard in the last hour; after a restart it waits until it knows, and says so. `hub_off` and the bypass labels do not look at this. Manual steps 70, 73 to 75 and 79.
  - **A goodbye that could not be sent is no longer forgotten or logged as sent.** The announcer remembers each address devices were told until a goodbye naming it really leaves or its 10 minutes run out, tries again every 20 seconds (while held, and after switching off, when the state stays "stopping" with a reason), and sends from the last announced card and address when the card has lost its own. The log says "Devices were told to stop" only when they were. Manual step 81.
  - **The announcer starts last.** `main()` starts it once the dashboard's port is open and a stop request is caught, inside the block whose clean-up sends the goodbye, so a start-up that fails announces nothing and Docker's restarts cannot keep renewing an announcement with no off switch.
  - **A card with two link-local addresses is not announced from:** answers could leave from the one devices were not told. New `link_locals_of()`; the page gives the reason. Manual step 80.
  - Tests: the two "at once" tests now start from the settled state, where they can fail; the test question is checked over IPv6 (a stand-in on `::1`, and the socket, address and card number used for a link-local address); the "starting" and "stopping" states and the empty address and time outside "announcing"; the sum that makes the clean-stop wait long enough; `main()` run with stand-ins for the start order.
- **Joined with the device tables and IPv6 steering (the second to fifth parts).** The trial still holds back while a Wi-Fi range extender is known. With the device tables in use the reason it gives is the true one: QuietNet would refuse those devices' IPv6 lookups or give them only the whole house's rules, no longer "could give one device another's rules", which now applies only to a database not yet moved. Whether the hold should come out for a moved database is left to the owner (issue 102).
- Joined with "Names a device's own system needs" (below): the trial adds no page, card or call that lists activity or blocks a name, so the question before blocking, the "Needed by ..." badge, the note beside a needed name the owner blocked and the `409` all work here as on the earlier parts. The trial does bring a new way of asking: a device that takes it up asks from its own link-local IPv6 address. A new test covers a needed name looked up that way: it is filed on the device's card and the list says who needs it. To make room for that change's issue 95 and manual check 16, and for the earlier parts' new numbers (96 to 101; steps 18 to 68), the trial's open issue is now 102 (it was 101 while this was being built), its manual steps moved up by one (they were 67 to 80; with step 17 since taken by the check of stopping, below, they are now 69 to 82) and the added step for a built-in IPv6 DNS server is 83 (it was 81). The tests that read issue 102 and step 82 out of the docs (`WhatTheDocsSay` in `tests/test_announcer.py`) follow.

Written down, not changed: the enforcer's wait (15 seconds) and the announcer's (5) add up to the whole of Docker's 20 seconds, though they overlap (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "How long the clean-up can take"; issue 102). The trial's manual steps moved up by one again (see the line above); manual step 17 here also points to step 74, the clean stop with the trial on, which now tries a stop during start-up too.
- With the fix for the two gaps in the tests merged in (25 November; see its entry below): the 15 tests that read this computer's network card from stand-in files (`ThisComputersCard` in `tests/test_announcer.py`) remove the folder each one makes, so a whole run on this part leaves nothing behind either.
- **Fixed in the tests: two start-up tests each left the other part's background job running.** The announcer's start-up tests ran `main()` and so started the real device keeper, which never ends, reads this computer's real neighbour table every 5 seconds and writes to the test database, disturbing later tests; the device tables' start-up test started a real announcer. Each now has a stand-in for the other, and a new test checks the announcer is started after the device keeper and last before the dashboard.

Take IPv6 away from steered devices (option c2 of the IPv6, DNS and device identity design; off by default):
- **New: an optional setting, "Take IPv6 away from steered devices"**, off unless the owner turns it on (`POST /api/ipv6-steer-switch`, `ipv6_steer` in `GET /api/status`). On, and only for devices "Cut live connections" is on for, QuietNet tells each of those devices that it, not the hub, is the way out for IPv6, and answers their lookups with no IPv6 addresses, so they use IPv4 where blocks and cuts already work. It needs no IPv6 forwarding and changes nothing lasting on the Pi.
- The claim is a neighbour advertisement for the hub's link-local address, sent only to a steered device's own hardware and link-local addresses (never the all-nodes group, never the hub's, the Pi's, a fixed or a range extender's address), built as whole frames with hop limit 255 and a correct ICMPv6 checksum, and re-sent every tick, whenever the device checks the hub is reachable, and whenever the hub announces itself. QuietNet answers those reachability checks from a raw socket and joins the multicast group they arrive on.
- **New: the firewall sends a steered device's IPv6 lookups for the hub's address to QuietNet** (an `ip6` "redirect DNS" chain, matching the device by hardware address). A device is claimed only when that redirect can really be loaded and answered: the `nft` tool is present, QuietNet is listening over IPv6, it has a lasting IPv6 address of its own, and the hub's IPv6 DNS address is known. Without all of those QuietNet leaves every device's IPv6 alone and the dashboard says why, so a steered device's lookups to the hub's IPv6 DNS address are never left silent. If an `nft` load with the `ip6` chain is rejected, QuietNet loads the rest without it (the IPv4 rules still land) and stops IPv6 claiming. The IPv6 listener answers each lookup from the address it was sent to (`IPV6_PKTINFO`), as the IPv4 one already did.
- **Devices behind a range extender are never IPv6-steered**, and their page says so; a hardware address with more than one link-local address is treated the same way. IPv6 follows IPv4 steering: only a device the enforcer is actually holding is claimed, and a device whose IPv4 steering is paused is handed back. A device's link-local address is learned from the Pi's neighbour table or from frames the Pi hears, so a device that has never spoken IPv6 to the Pi can still be steered; a chosen device keeps its claim at its last known address if the table loses it.
- Turning the setting off is also "Stop IPv6 steering now": every steered device is handed straight back to the hub (a correction with the hub's real hardware address) within the stop grace period, and `poke()` runs it at once. Which devices are steered, and the hub's addresses the claims were made against, are saved in `enforcer_state.json`, so the first tick after a restart hands them back before the hub is heard again; devices still chosen are claimed again once the hub next announces.
- A reachability check is answered to the device's own recorded link-local address whatever source it asked from (a device resolving the hub because of ordinary traffic asks from its public address), with a few delayed repeats so the Pi's answer beats the hub's.
- **Inert while off**: the listener holds no socket and joins no group, the IPv6 tick reads no neighbour table of its own (it reuses the IPv4 tick's), and a hub announcement wakes the enforcer only while the setting is on, at most once a second. IPv6 forwarding being on is treated as a shown problem that stops the claims (taking IPv6 away would then stop nothing). An IPv6 address naming a network card after a `%` is refused before it can reach a firewall rule.
- `GET /api/status`'s `enforcer` part gains `ipv6_steering` (`offered`, `enabled`, `steered`, `hub_known`, `problem`, `problems`); `offered` is false (alone) without the `nft` tool. For a household that leaves the setting off, the firewall text and the enforcer behave exactly as before. One thing changes for everyone, on or off: on Linux the IPv6 listener now answers each lookup from the address it was sent to, where before the system chose; if the system won't send from that address the answer goes out the old way.
- **Which device is steered comes from the device tables**, as for IPv4 (joined with the second to fourth parts of the design): only a device with "Cut live connections" on that the live neighbour table lists, at the number its rules are held at and with its own hardware address. No such entry, no claim; never from the record of who used an address. A device merged from two hardware addresses is claimed at each. A device known only by its number, one behind a range extender, and one whose hardware address is at two live numbers at once are left alone and their page says why. `plan6` goes by the neighbour table the IPv4 plan read on the tick before, reads it itself (before taking the database lock) only when there is none, and while the setting is off names no device and reads nothing.
- Joined with "Names a device's own system needs" (below): this part changes neither the activity list nor any way of blocking a name, so the question before blocking, the "Needed by ..." badge, the note beside a needed name the owner blocked and the `409` all work here as on the earlier parts, on every card. To make room for that change's issue 95 and manual check 16, and for the earlier parts' new numbers (96 to 100; steps 18 to 60), this part's open issue is now 101 (it was 100 while this was being built) and its manual steps moved up by one (they were 59 to 66; with step 17 since taken by the check of stopping, below, they are now 61 to 68).
- With the stop-request fix merged in (25 November; see its entry below): `start_up()` starts what `main()` started, in the same order, and still hands the enforcer `plan6()` as well as its plan. IPv6 steering is the enforcer's own, begun on its tick and undone by its `stop()`, so this part adds no line to `start_up()` or `clean_up()`: the clean-up's wait for the enforcer now covers the handing back of a steered device's IPv6, which goes first, as well as its IPv4. Three new tests in `tests/test_start_stop.py` (46 in all) check that the enforcer is handed `plan6`, that an enforcer made with it but never started is stopped without asking for a plan, and, with the real enforcer holding a phone and a stand-in for everything it would touch, that "QuietNet stopped." is printed only after the phone's IPv6 and IPv4 have both been handed back. One test of the lookup listener that came with the fix now switches off the IPv6 "which address was this sent to" option, as this part's other tests with a stand-in socket do. Found on the way, and written under issue 101: with the setting on, the enforcer's last act needs the database lock. This part's manual steps moved up by one again (see the line above), and manual step 17 here ends by pointing to step 66, the check of a stop with IPv6 steering on.
- This is built but has not run on a real Pi. `docs/TESTING.md` steps 61 to 68 (and 83, added later) list what must be checked there; what it leaves open is issue 101.

One card per device, Merge and Separate (fourth part of the IPv6, DNS and device identity design; 24 November):
- **New: one card per device on the dashboard.** A card is known by the device's number, no longer by an address. It shows the number the device has now, a count of its other addresses ("and 3 IPv6 addresses"), and its hardware address or addresses. A device that has left, and whose number went to another device, now has a card too ("Not on your network now"), so its name, rules and history can be seen and changed. A device known only by its number says "Recognised by its number"; the router is called "Your router" until it is named.
- **New: the device's page lists its addresses**, each with when it was first and last seen there and whether it is there now, and its hardware addresses.
- **New: "Merge with" on a device's page** (`POST /api/merge`), for one device that shows as two: a laptop on cable and Wi-Fi, or a phone with a new private Wi-Fi address. Nothing is merged automatically, and nothing is changed by the first request: the answer is a preview of every rule that would apply to each hardware address afterwards, marking what is new for it and what stops. Only a second request that sends the preview's marker back merges, and only if the preview would still read the same (`409` otherwise). A device with rules of its own can now be merged, which the older call refused because it could not show the result.
- **New: "Separate"** (`POST /api/separate`) takes one hardware address out of a merged device as a device of its own. It is previewed the same way, and asks whether the rules go with it: a copy of the group, internet switch, blocked services, schedules and "cut live connections", or nothing. There is no default. Its history so far stays with the device it was merged into, and the two are remembered as different devices, so the separated one is not at once held to the other's rules as "maybe the same device".
- **Changed: "Is it the same device?" goes through the same preview.** "Yes" on a notice now shows what would apply before joining the two. `POST /api/move-rules` is kept for pages written before, and the page falls back to it for an older Pi reached through the relay.
- **Changed: pickers list devices by name**, in name order (the schedule form, "Merge with", a group's page); an address is added only to tell two devices with the same name apart.
- **Changed: a group QuietNet itself put a device in as a new device gives way when that device is merged**, whichever of the two it is; a group the owner chose does not.
- **Changed: `GET /api/devices` also lists devices the owner has merged, separated or said are different**, so what was just done shows before the device next looks anything up. The page asks with `?all=1`.
- **Fixed: the page could ask to clear everyone's history** if it ever had neither a number nor an address for the open device. It now sends nothing in that case.
- Docs: `docs/API.md` has "Merge and Separate"; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` describes the preview and the cards; `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` has what was closed and the new open issue 100; `docs/TESTING.md` has manual steps 49 to 56. New tests: `tests/test_merge_separate.py`.
- Joined with "Names a device's own system needs" (below): a card is known by the device's number, and its activity list still carries `needed` on every name and group, so the page shows "Needed by ...", asks before blocking, and shows its note beside a needed name the owner blocked, on the cards this part added too: a device that is not on the network now (the page asks with an empty address and the device's number) and a merged device (one list for both hardware addresses). Two new tests cover those. On the page a refused call now carries both its status, which Merge and Separate read, and its answer, which the question before blocking reads. To make room for that change's issue 95 and manual check 16, and for the earlier parts' new numbers (96 to 99; steps 18 to 48), this part's open issue is now 100 (it was 99 while this was being built) and its manual steps moved up by one (they were 47 to 58; with step 17 since taken by the check of stopping, below, they are now 49 to 60).

This part's manual steps moved up by one again (see the line above).

Faults found in the check of the Merge and Separate build, fixed before release (24 November):
- **Fixed: Separate could let a hardware address out from under rules it was being held to.** A device still being asked about ("same device?", not answered) that had another device merged into it could be separated again with the open question left on the wrong one of the two: the child's phone came out with no rules and the laptop was held to the child's. The Separate preview now shows what reaches the device through an open question (`held` and `follows` in `POST /api/separate`'s preview) and says it goes on applying to both; the separated device is held to the same rules, and the question is asked about each. Nothing is newly blocked.
- **Fixed: the merge preview no longer says a merge with a device known only by its number can be undone with Separate.** It can't (a number has no hardware address to take out again), and the preview, the text under "Merge with" and the documents now say so, and say that the way back is to take the rules off the merged device by hand. Two devices that each have a hardware address can still be taken apart with Separate, and the preview still says that.
- **Fixed: a number behind a Wi-Fi range extender could be merged while the extender was not listed** (its device asleep or away, or just after a restart), which moved its rules to a device that can never be matched behind the extender. QuietNet now remembers which numbers an extender answers for (a small new table, `behind_extender`, made by itself on an existing database) and refuses the merge by that. The refusal lifts when the extender is forgotten (30 days not seen sharing) or when a device known by its own hardware address is seen at the number.
- **Fixed: a confirm could be refused with "Something changed since this was shown" when nothing had.** The preview's marker no longer depends on which address an unnamed device happens to be shown by, which changes from look to look for a merged laptop with both cards connected. A changed rule, name, group or hardware address still makes a preview out of date.
- **Fixed: the Schedules page could name a merged device by a bare address** when both its cards were connected. The page now names a schedule's device by its number, and `GET /api/devices` lists a device at two addresses confirmed at the same moment under the lower one, the same address a schedule's scope and messages name.
- **Changed: a Pi with device numbers but no Merge yet** (the second or third part, reached through a relay serving this page) is told "This QuietNet needs updating before devices can be merged" (or separated) when either is pressed, in place of "Something went wrong. Is QuietNet still running?".
- Docs: the README and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` say the device's page lists the addresses of the last day, 32 at most, not every address; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` no longer describes the "yes" to "same device?" as `POST /api/move-rules`; `docs/API.md` lists the device limit among the refusals of `POST /api/separate`; `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` has the fixed faults and what is left under issue 100; `docs/TESTING.md` has manual steps 57 to 60 and a corrected step 55. New tests for each, and for four behaviours that had none (the rules a kept device is itself held to in the merge preview, the group kept when merging into a number-only entry, a Separate request with no answer about the rules, and two refusals).

Rules held against the device (third part of the IPv6, DNS and device identity design; 24 November):
- **New: a device's name, group and rules belong to the device, whatever number it has.** They are kept by device number: the name in `device.name`, the group in `group_member`, "cut live connections" in `live_cut`, and its own internet switch, blocked services and schedules under the scope `dev:<number>`. Nothing is moved when a device is given a new address, over IPv4 or IPv6. The once-a-minute pass that moved rules from address to address is gone (`move_rules()`, `S.follow`, the pause on an address).
- **New: whose rules a lookup gets** is one dictionary lookup in `S.addr_rules`, made before `decision()`, which still touches no database.
